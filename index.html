The researchers wanted to know whether giving voters reliable information about politicians would change their votes.

1. They ran the study in several places.

Benin, Brazil, Burkina Faso, Mexico, and Uganda (two studies).

Who received the information was randomized. This makes it possible to estimate the effect of the campaign.

2. They made the plan together, before starting.

The researchers worked together through the Metaketa Initiative. Before they saw the results, they agreed on the key things that would make the studies comparable.

🤝
Same main questionDoes information change support for the politician or party already in office?
🗳️
Same main outcomeDid voters support the current politician or ruling party? This is called incumbent support.
📝
Plan the analysis and reportingAgree on the comparisons in advance and include results that aren't statistically significant.
They didn't run identical studies.
Brazil could share information about local spending, while Benin could share information about lawmakers' work. The core plan stayed similar.

Ex ante coordination = agree before starting. Preregistration = write the plan down before looking at the data. Meta-analysis = analyze the studies together.

3. The expected result didn't show up.

If voters learned good news, researchers expected more support for the current politician. With bad news, they expected less.

Good news → incumbent support+0.62 ppNo clear overall effect
Bad news → incumbent support+0.36 ppNo clear overall effect

pp = percentage points. Both estimates were statistically indistinguishable from zero. There was also no clear overall effect on turnout.

The information campaigns did not clearly change how people voted.

That doesn't prove the effect was exactly zero. It means the researchers didn't find convincing evidence of an overall effect from these kinds of campaigns.

4. Why is a result like this still useful?

If we only paid attention to studies with positive results, we might think information campaigns always work. This project made room for the studies that found little or no effect, too.

What can we learn from this paper?

🤝

Agree before you start.

Decide on a shared plan before running the studies.

📝

Don't cherry-pick.

Don't show only the results that look good.

📊

Look at all the evidence.

Use findings from all the studies, not just one.

This is why the researchers used ex ante coordination: they made a shared plan before their studies began.

The lesson isn't “information never works.” It's “we need the full evidence before we decide.”
